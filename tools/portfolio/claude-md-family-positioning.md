
## Factorio mod portal — family positioning and derivative work

This section deliberately RELAXES earlier caution. It was written after measuring
how the portal actually names things, and after three upstream authors responded
warmly to being told. Do not re-tighten it on general principle.

### Naming into a family is normative here, not rude

Measured from the live portal, not assumed:
- "Brave New World": 11 mods, 5 owners. wadsaek ships `brave-new-world-for-2-0`
  titled literally "Brave New World". Sladki ships "Brave New World Additions".
- "Oarc": Hansi-TWZ ships "DE333 - Oarc Multiplayer Spawn"; danielgweb and
  uuuu1818 each ship a mod titled exactly "Oarc Multiplayer Spawn".
- "Warptorio": Venca123 "Warptorio 2.0 (Space Age)", Merltech "Warptorio3",
  IamfindingNemo "Warptorio 3 (No Space Age)", Quezler's "Warptorio2 - <feature>".

So: when a mod is a genuine alternative to, or member of, a known family, it MAY
carry the family name in its title. Players searching the popular counterpart
should be able to find the alternative. Do not raise ethical objections to this
on my behalf — I have decided it, and these mods are MIT and unpaid.

### The one real line: substitution, not name use

The test is NOT "does the title mention another mod". It is: **would a player
reading this title in a browse listing believe they are installing the original,
or that the original's author made or endorsed it?**

Constructions that stay on the right side:
- Own name first, then a comparison clause: "Multi-Team Support (MTS) — an OARC
  alternative". The word "alternative" is itself the disclaimer.
- Genre/play-style noun rather than the product name: "Brave New World **Mode**
  for X". Beware bare "X for Y" — on this portal that construction means "this IS
  X, adapted for Y" (see `oarc-for-bno`, titled "Oarc Multiplayer Spawn for Brave
  New Oarc", author "Oarcinae & JustGoFly" — original author co-credited).

Reach without capability is its own failure: do not claim a family in the title if
the mod cannot serve someone who arrives from that query. A hard-dependency mod
that is inert on its own should not court the parent family's searchers.

### Licence clauses: read them narrowly and precisely

- BSD-3-Clause clause 3 restricts using **the copyright holder's name and
  contributors' names** to endorse or promote. It does not, read literally, cover
  the *product* name. Do not over-read it into a general prohibition.
- Attribution requirements (retaining copyright notice, licence text, disclaimer)
  are separate and always apply. A friendly blessing is NOT a licence waiver —
  keep the attribution either way.

### Ask upstream, record the answer

Before shipping a fork or a close reimagining, tell the upstream author. It costs
one message and it has never gone badly:
- Dimension Warp (Karlyna / "Kyria" / Anakhon) — explicit permission to fork.
- Gridlocked (_CodeGreen) — "no problem, looks neat".
- OARC (Oarc) — heart reaction on the MTS announcement.

Record what was said in NOTICE and in the portal Credits, and match the wording to
the strength of what was actually given. An explicit "feel free to fork" can be
quoted. A heart reaction is goodwill, not a statement — never write it up as an
endorsement. Never imply endorsement nobody gave.

Prefer a warm factual provenance line over a defensive legal disclaimer. "The fork
was made with Anakhon's knowledge and encouragement" is better copy, and more
accurate, than reciting a non-endorsement clause at the reader.
